## Product decisions

### Label facts

Use the actual ingredient quantities, serving size, directions and warnings. Distinguish amount per serving from amount per container.

### Evidence

Separate ingredient research, formulation evidence and evidence about the sold product. A study of an ingredient does not automatically substantiate a product outcome.

### Suitability

Show relevant restrictions and intended users from the verified label or merchant evidence. Never invent a clinical promise or omit a material limitation to shorten the pitch.

### Image decisions

Prioritize label readability, packaging identity, serving scale and real product detail. Research diagrams illustrate a supported explanation; they do not become proof by appearing scientific.

### Purchase options

Bind pack size, quantity tiers and selling plans to catalogue data. Explain duration and commitments from the actual serving and plan information.

### Proof context

Use the proof owners for certification, test reports, reviews and before/after claims. Do not treat badges, white coats or numbers as interchangeable trust decoration.
